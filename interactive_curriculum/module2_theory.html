Core Reinforcement Learning Theory & Math

First-principles deep dive: Formal MDP mechanics, Why Q-learning beats Policy Gradients here, Implicit Quantile Networks (IQN), Quantile Huber Loss derivation, and the validity of undiscounted \(\gamma = 1.0\).

01. INTUITION

The GPS Average vs The Distribution of Traffic

🗺️

Why Scalar Expected Values (\(\mathbb{E}[R]\)) Fail in High-Speed Racing

Imagine Google Maps tells you: "Route A takes 20 minutes on average. Route B takes 20 minutes on average."
Standard Q-learning (DQN) considers both routes identical because \(Q(s, a_A) = Q(s, a_B) = 20\).

The reality: Route A has mild stop-and-go traffic (variance: 19 to 21 mins). Route B is an empty mountain cliff with a 10% chance of a catastrophic boulder landslide that traps you for 10 hours! In TrackMania at 400 km/h, taking an aggressive drift line has a 90% chance of gaining 100m, but a 10% chance of clipping the curb by 1 pixel, flipping upside down, and ending your run. Standard DQN averages them into a flat number. Distributional RL maps the entire shape of the outcome.

02. FORMAL MDP

The Markov Decision Process \((S, A, P, R, \gamma)\)

Every RL problem is formally specified as a 5-tuple \((S, A, P, R, \gamma)\). Here is how Project Linesight instantiates every element mathematically:

👁️
State Space \(S\)
Multi-Modal State:
• Grayscale visual frame: \(160 \times 120 \times 1\)
• Car Telemetry: Speed scalar, engine RPM, steer angle, 4-wheel surface contact flags (Ice, Grass, Road, Air).
• Centerline Preview: 40 upcoming 3D coordinates spaced 10m apart (400m horizon) transformed to car local frame.
🎮
Action Space \(A\)
12 Discrete Actions:
Cartesian combinations of keys: Steer \(\in \{\text{Left, Right, None}\}\) \(\times\) Throttle \(\in \{\text{Gas, Brake, Coast}\}\).
Held for 5 physics engine ticks (\(5 \times 10\text{ms} = 50\text{ms}\) per action).
⚡
Transition Probability \(P\)
Deterministic Physics:
\(P(s_{t+1} \mid s_t, a_t) = 1.0\).
TrackMania's custom C++ engine is 100% deterministic down to the IEEE 754 floating-point calculation. The environment introduces no stochastic environmental noise.
🎯
Reward Function \(R\)
Centerline Progress - Time:
\(r_t = \Delta \text{distance}_{\text{centerline}} - c \cdot \Delta t\).
Plus speedslide bonus multiplier when lateral drift angle matches optimal tire slip friction curves.
THE MATHEMATICAL JUSTIFICATION FOR \(\gamma = 1.0\) EPISODIC INTEGRITY
\[R_t = \sum_{k=0}^{T - t} \gamma^k r_{t+k} \quad \xrightarrow{\gamma = 1.0} \quad R_t = \sum_{k=0}^{T - t} r_{t+k}\]
Why isn't \(\gamma=1.0\) blowing up to infinity?
In textbook RL (Sutton & Barto), \(\gamma < 1.0\) is required for continuing (infinite-horizon) tasks to guarantee the geometric series \(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \gamma^k r_{t+k}\) converges to a finite bound. However, TrackMania is strictly an episodic, finite-horizon task: every run terminates either when crossing the finish line (\(T \approx 54\text{s}\)) or reaching a timeout cutoff (\(T \le 300\text{s}\)). Setting \(\gamma = 1.0\) ensures that progress earned 30 seconds into the lap is valued equally to progress earned in Turn 1, directly aligning cumulative return with minimizing total lap time without artificial temporal decay!
03. ARCHITECTURE CHOICE

Why Q-Learning over Policy Gradients (PPO / SAC)?

Senior interviewers will ask: "PPO is the modern default for continuous robotics; why did you use an off-policy Q-learning method?"

Dimension On-Policy Policy Gradients (PPO) Off-Policy Distributional Q-Learning (Linesight IQN)
Sample Efficiency ❌ Very Low: Rollouts collected by policy \(\pi_\theta\) must be discarded after a few gradient updates. In a 10M-frame run, 99% of data is incinerated. ✓ Extremely High: Experiences \((s, a, r, s', done)\) enter a 200,000-transition replay buffer and are sampled hundreds of times for gradient updates.
Action Space Compatibility Designed primarily for continuous Gaussians (\(\mu, \sigma\)). Discrete softmax policies can struggle with exploration collapse in tight timing windows. ✓ Natural Fit: Evaluates discrete actions via \(a^* = \arg\max_a Q(s, a)\). Directly matches discrete keyboard keystrokes.
Simulation Coupling Requires lockstep synchronous actor-critic updates. Fast sim speeds cause learners to bottleneck collectors. ✓ Asynchronous Decoupling: Collectors run at 80× speed writing to shared memory buffer while the Learner executes uninterrupted GPU/CPU batches.
04. DISTRIBUTIONAL RL

Implicit Quantile Networks (IQN): Dabney et al. (2018)

Instead of learning the scalar expectation \(Q(s, a) = \mathbb{E}[Z(s, a)]\), IQN models the return distribution \(Z(s, a)\) directly using continuous quantile regression.

COSINE QUANTILE EMBEDDING EQUATION FOURIER BASIS EXPANSION
\[\phi_j(\tau) = \text{ReLU}\left( \sum_{i=0}^{d-1} w_{ji} \cos(\pi \cdot i \cdot \tau) + b_j \right) \quad \text{for } \tau \sim U(0, 1)\]
Why Cosine Basis Functions?
A scalar quantile fraction \(\tau \in [0, 1]\) cannot be easily multiplied with a high-dimensional state vector without causing vanishing representation. By projecting \(\tau\) through frequencies \(\cos(\pi i \tau)\) for \(i \in \{1, \dots, 32\}\), IQN creates a rich frequency embedding that the dense layers can combine with visual/telemetry features via Hadamard (elementwise) multiplication: \[\text{feature} = \text{concat}(x_{\text{conv}}, x_{\text{telemetry}}) \odot \text{embedding}(\tau)\]

05. LOSS FUNCTION

The Quantile Huber Loss: Derivation & Pinball Geometry

⚖️

The Pinball Asymmetric Penalty Intuition

Standard Mean Squared Error (MSE) penalizes overestimating and underestimating identically: \((y - \hat{y})^2\). This mathematically forces the prediction to the Mean (50th percentile).
If you want the network to predict the 90th percentile (\(\tau = 0.9\)), you must punish underestimating 9 times harder than overestimating! That asymmetric penalty is the Pinball Loss: \(|\tau - \mathbb{I}(\delta < 0)|\).

THE COMPLETE QUANTILE HUBER LOSS DABNEY ET AL., 2018
\[\rho_\tau^\kappa(\delta) = |\tau - \mathbb{I}(\delta < 0)| \cdot \frac{\mathcal{L}_\kappa(\delta)}{\kappa}\]
Where the Huber Loss \(\mathcal{L}_\kappa(\delta)\) is defined as: \[\mathcal{L}_\kappa(\delta) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{2} \delta^2 & \text{if } |\delta| \le \kappa \\ \kappa \left( |\delta| - \frac{1}{2} \kappa \right) & \text{if } |\delta| > \kappa \end{cases}\]
Why smooth with Huber Loss?
The standard Pinball loss has a non-differentiable kink at \(\delta = 0\), causing gradient oscillation when predictions get close to targets. The threshold \(\kappa\) (set to \(1.0\)) makes the loss quadratic for small errors and linear for large errors, preventing gradient explosion from extreme racing crashes!
