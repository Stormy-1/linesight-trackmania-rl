Why Shaving 240ms in Late-Stage Racing Is Exponentially Harder Than 10 Seconds

When an agent starts learning from scratch, it doesn't even know how to hold the gas pedal. Any random action that stays on the track yields thousands of reward points. Shaving 10 seconds is trivial because the error landscape is massive.

At 54.000s, the car is driving at 99.56% of the human World Record (53.760s). It is executing 1,080 discrete actions across 54 seconds.
The entire 240ms gap represents just ~4.8 discrete actions (at 50ms per action)!

The Exploration Bottleneck: Shaving those 4.8 actions requires a tightly coordinated sequence (e.g. initiating a drift 50ms earlier, holding counter-steer for 100ms, and accelerating out cleanly). Random \(\epsilon\)-exploration perturbs only single, isolated actions. The odds of uncorrelated exploration discovering a 3-step coordinated sequence by chance is \(\epsilon^3 = 0.03^3 = 0.000027\) (roughly 1 in 37,000 attempts)!

Why We Branch from `save/hocko_v1_foundational_checkpoint/` (~54.45s):

At 3.18M frames (54.45s), the agent reached ~95% performance on Hockolicious before hyper-specializing to track-specific micro-timings. Note: Linesight uses LeakyReLU ($a=0.01$), so neurons do not mathematically 'die' as in standard ReLU, but representation contraction and weight saturation can still occur. We branch from this checkpoint under the hypothesis that it preserves higher adaptability, which we benchmark directly against the saturated 10.64M model.
